# Crew only. Here's how we check.

## Why
Galley is for pilots and cabin crew: plans at your base on your days off, and company on a layover. Saying yes to a plan is easy when you know everyone there flies too. So every account is checked before it can see anyone.

## Who can join
Pilots and cabin crew flying now, or in airline new-hire training. Any airline, anywhere.

Not ground staff, dispatchers or other airline staff, family or buddy-pass holders, retired crew, student pilots, aviation fans or recruiters.

## How to prove it

<div class="card"><strong>1. A code to your work email</strong>Enter your airline email, get a 6-digit code, type it in. Usually under a minute. One email from Galley lands in your work inbox; that's all your airline's mail system sees.</div>
<div class="card"><strong>2. Two colleagues vouch</strong>No work email on your phone? Two crew from your airline who joined with a work email vouch for you in the app. Their own accounts answer for it.</div>

No screenshots, no crew ID, no passport, no staff number. Ever.

First time we see your airline's email domain? A person checks the domain once, usually within 24 hours. After that, everyone from it gets straight in.

## What we keep
A one-way code made from your work address, never the address itself. It stops one mailbox opening two accounts and keeps a banned account from coming back. The address is deleted as soon as you type the code, or after 15 minutes. We also keep the domain and the decision with its date.

## Keeping it crew only
Once a year you confirm your work email again. When someone leaves their airline, their mailbox goes and so do they.

An airline email shows that someone works at an airline, not that they fly. So anyone can report a profile as "not crew". That person gets 72 hours to show us two small crops from their roster (their name, and one day flown in the last 2 weeks), cropped on their phone so nothing else leaves it, or to do a short video call. They stay visible while they answer. If they don't, or it doesn't check out, they're hidden until it does. They never learn who asked.

You answer for who you bring in. One rejected invite turns your invite link off; three put you back under review. If someone you vouched for turns out not to be crew, your vouching and invite link switch off.

## Invite-only at first
We open base by base. While yours is invite-only, you get in with a crew member's invite link, a code from us, or two vouches. No code? Verify anyway and you're on the list. We open seats in waves and tell you when you're in.

## What verification is not
Verification shows that someone is crew. It can't tell you what they're like as a person, so meet people the way you'd meet any new colleague. Here are some [tips for meeting safely](/safety/).
